## What these photographs actually show, and why it matters

**These are lower fan leaves, and the pattern is contested.** Llewellyn et al.
observed calcium deficiency symptoms "starting on lower fan leaves", and say so
explicitly: "Since Ca is normally considered an immobile nutrient, these
observed symptoms are also a departure from typical calcium deficiency in
terrestrial plants, where foliar symptoms normally start in younger tissues."
Their tissue data sharpens the oddity: lower-canopy foliage held almost three
times the calcium of the upper canopy at symptom onset.

Cockson et al. (2019, *Applied Sciences* 9(20), 4432, also CC BY) found the
textbook pattern instead, in vegetative plants: "The growing tips and newly
expanding leaves showed signs of stunting and irregular growth habits", with
marginal necrosis on newly expanding leaves and, in advanced cases, a dead
terminal tip.

So two peer-reviewed cannabis studies document two different presentations. The
article's `p51` was rewritten to say that rather than assert one of them, and
the figure caption names which study it depicts. **Do not recaption this figure
as "calcium deficiency appears in new growth."** It shows the opposite pattern,
from the paper that reported it.

An earlier version of this figure used Cockson's calcium photographs instead.
They were dropped for image quality: Figure 2 there is a three by three grid, so
each sub-panel is only about 794 px wide, and no higher-resolution original
exists (the plate is 2718 px in both the web PNG and the article PDF, checked
with `pdfimages -list`). Enlarging them only made the softness legible.
